This operating system treats insurance as one component of household resilience. Build a private household model, understand policy structure, plan for deductibles and disruptions, maintain what you own, document what matters, and rehearse recovery before a loss occurs.

Educational and planning tool — not an insurer, broker, adjuster, attorney, financial adviser, contractor, or claims representative.Policy wording, local rules, eligibility, assistance programs and hazard conditions can change. Verify consequential decisions with official sources and qualified professionals. Insurance does not eliminate risk.

Warning signs

  • Demand for full upfront payment.
  • High-pressure “today only” tactics.
  • Vague or changing written scope.
  • Refusal to provide written documentation.
  • Unsolicited post-disaster solicitation.
  • Requests to misstate damage or claim details.

Disaster documentation mode

Safety first. Then create a durable record.

Post-event sequence

  1. Address immediate life safety and follow public-safety instructions.
  2. Photograph/video damage from safe positions before cleanup where practical.
  3. Prevent further damage only when safe and within your policy obligations.
  4. Record affected rooms, systems and items.
  5. Contact the insurer using verified contact information.
  6. Save receipts for emergency work and temporary needs.
  7. Track calls, emails, names, dates, estimates and submitted documents.

Interpretation guardrails

  • Average premiums are statewide/county market context, not quotes.
  • Paid-loss history is not a forecast of a household’s future loss.
  • Weather alerts are official NWS products but users must follow local authorities and the full alert text.
  • FEMA declaration status does not by itself establish individual assistance eligibility.
  • Flood maps/plans do not capture every mechanism or future condition.
